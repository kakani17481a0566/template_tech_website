From Parents

A Mother’s First Teacher

When Meera became a mother for the first time, she believed that education would begin when her daughter entered preschool. Then she attended a RSES (now called Mission 2000) parent workshop. She discovered that every smile, every lullaby, every conversation, every hug, every bedtime story, and every moment of play was already shaping her daughter’s brain and future.

That evening, Meera switched off her phone, sat on the floor, and spent an hour talking, singing, and laughing with her little girl. It seemed like an ordinary evening. But it was the beginning of something extraordinary.

Years later, Meera would say: “RSES (now called Mission 2000) didn’t teach my daughter first. It taught me how to become her first teacher.”

Sometimes, changing a nation begins by changing one evening in one home.

Stories from Teachers

The Teacher Who Changed a Village

In a small village, preschool teacher Kavitha welcomed twenty-three children every morning. Her classroom had few toys. Her walls were simple. But her imagination had no limits.

She turned leaves into counting games. Stones became mathematics. Songs became language lessons. Stories became lessons in kindness.

Parents soon noticed that their children were happier, more curious, and more confident. Within two years, neighbouring villages began visiting her classroom to learn from her.

When someone asked Kavitha how she had transformed the school, she smiled and replied: “I never tried to change a school. I simply decided that every child who entered my classroom deserved my very best.”

Great nations are built by teachers who quietly change one child at a time.

Stories from Schools

Discover inspiring stories from schools, teachers, and communities that are transforming early childhood through innovation, inclusion, creativity, and compassion. Every story demonstrates how small actions can create lasting national impact.

The School That Opened Its Doors

A preschool decided that once every month, parents would spend an hour learning alongside their children.

They painted together. Built towers together. Read stories together. Laughed together.

Teachers stopped being seen as people who replaced parents. They became partners.

The school discovered something remarkable. Children whose families participated became more confident, more engaged, and more excited about learning.

Education had moved beyond the classroom. It had become a shared journey.

The Doctor Who Prescribed Play

Dr. Rahman had spent twenty years caring for children. Parents expected prescriptions. Instead, one young family received unexpected advice. “Read together every day.” “Talk while you cook.” “Play on the floor.” “Sing songs.” “Look into your baby’s eyes when you speak.”

The parents looked surprised. “Doctor, where is the medicine?”

He smiled. “Sometimes the greatest medicine for a growing brain is love, conversation, and play.”

Mission 2000 reminds us that healthy childhood is built through both medical care and meaningful relationships.

Research into Practice

Bringing the latest insights from neuroscience, child development, education, nutrition, and psychology into simple, practical ideas that parents, teachers, and caregivers can use every day to help children thrive.

The Power of Just Fifteen Minutes

When little Aarav joined preschool, his teachers noticed that he was easily distracted and rarely spoke during group activities. His parents worried that he was “not ready” for school and wondered if they should start extra classes.

Instead, his teacher introduced a simple daily routine based on research in early childhood development. Every evening, Aarav’s parents spent just 15 uninterrupted minutes with him. There were no mobile phones, no television, and no worksheets. They simply read a story, asked questions, sang songs, played simple games, and talked about the day’s experiences.

Within a few months, remarkable changes began to appear. Aarav’s vocabulary expanded, he became more confident in expressing his thoughts, and his attention span improved. Most importantly, he began to enjoy learning.

What changed? Not the child—but the experiences surrounding the child.

Research in neuroscience tells us that young brains develop through responsive relationships, meaningful conversations, play, movement, and emotional security. These everyday interactions strengthen the neural connections that support language, thinking, self-regulation, creativity, and lifelong learning.

Science does not always require expensive technology or complicated programmes. Sometimes, it begins with something as simple as 15 minutes of intentional connection every day.

That is the essence of Research into Practice—transforming scientific evidence into practical actions that every parent, teacher, and caregiver can use to help children flourish.

Mission 2000 believes that when science reaches every family, every child receives a stronger beginning—and India builds a stronger future.

The India We Are Building

Imagine an India…

Where every expecting mother receives support.

Where every father knows how important his voice is to his baby’s development.

Where every Anganwadi worker is celebrated.

Where every early childhood educator is respected as a nation builder.

Where every village has joyful places for children to play and learn.

Where science guides policy.
